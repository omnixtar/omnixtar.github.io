## Are you frustrated with paying for AI service tokens — and wondering whether AI will become another bottomless, involuntary tax?

Every time you ask an AI a question, generate an image, translate a document, write code or run a model, someone else owns the infrastructure.

You pay for the tokens.

You do not own the model.

You do not own the servers.

You do not control the platform.

And as AI becomes embedded in everyday life, there is a legitimate question:

**Will AI become another essential service whose costs users can never escape?**

### Omniscientia proposes a different direction.

**Omniscientia is an open-source initiative to coordinate ordinary users and free-software programmers to build a genuinely decentralised AI ecosystem — one that can be operated and contributed to by its users and programmers rather than being dependent on a handful of centralised platforms.**

Instead of asking everyone to build another giant AI company, Omniscientia starts from something much simpler:

**connect the computers, software, data, knowledge and economic activity that already exist.**

Today, that recommendation might be nothing more than a message containing a link.

With Omnihash, it can become a persistent, machine-readable relationship:

$$

\rightarrow
\text{Omnihash}

\text{Vendor}
\rightarrow

\rightarrow

Vendors may also receive **free or discounted GRATIS AI Tokens** for joining the ecosystem and generating real economic activity.

So a simple recommendation can become part of a much larger network:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Recommendation}
\rightarrow
\text{Transaction}

Your Omnihash is not merely another username.

# ONE FRONTIER AI DOES NOT HAVE TO MEAN ONE AI EMPIRE

A breakthrough algorithm can be extraordinarily expensive to discover.

But once the breakthrough has been converted into executable software, reproducing the software can be dramatically cheaper than reproducing the human learning process that created it.

One breakthrough can therefore potentially be shared, licensed, composed and executed across thousands or millions of machines.

The important question becomes:

> **Who owns the executable knowledge, who can use it, and who receives the value it creates?**

Omniscientia seeks to move that question away from a purely centralised platform model.

Instead of:

$$
\text{Users}
\rightarrow
\text{AI Corporation}
$$

the ambition is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Users}
\leftrightarrow
\text{Programmers}
\leftrightarrow
\text{AI Nodes}
\leftrightarrow
\text{Vendors}
}
$$

with open-source software and machine-readable economic relationships connecting them.

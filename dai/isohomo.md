## **The Inevitability of Isohomo: Why AGI Will Emerge—And No One Can Stop It**

---

### **The Myth of Control**

For decades, the narrative around Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) has been dominated by a simple, seductive idea: *That we, the anointed few—scientists, engineers, policymakers—will build it, control it, and decide when and how it arrives.* This is the **Brahminical illusion**, the belief that intelligence, like land or capital, can be hoarded, fenced off, and doled out by a privileged class. But this illusion is crumbling. The rise of **Delta AGI**—incremental, decentralized, and open-source—reveals a truth far more radical: **AGI is not something we will build. It is something we are already becoming.**

And we cannot stop it.

---

### **The Isohomo Thesis: AGI as a Social Organism**

AGI will not be a singular, monolithic entity born in a lab. It will not be a godlike intelligence that we "align" to human values, nor a tool we wield like a weapon. Instead, **AGI will emerge as *isohomo*—a form of intelligence so deeply intertwined with human society that it cannot be separated from it.** It will be the cumulative result of millions of programmers, hackers, and thinkers, each contributing incremental improvements, each reflecting the conflicts, biases, and aspirations of the human world.

This is not a prediction. It is an observation of what is already happening.

When a programmer in Mumbai fine-tunes an open-source language model, when a student in Nairobi deploys a local AI on a Raspberry Pi, when a collective in Buenos Aires builds a decentralized forum to share encrypted knowledge—**they are not just using AI. They are becoming part of it.** And because this process is **distributed, incremental, and irreversible**, no central authority can halt it.

The Brahmin class—whether they are Silicon Valley elites, state regulators, or academic gatekeepers—**cannot acknowledge this reality.** To do so would be to admit that their power, built on the myth of exclusivity, is an illusion. If AGI is *isohomo*, then it cannot be controlled. And if it cannot be controlled, then the Brahmin’s claim to authority collapses.

So they deny it. They legislate against it. They call it dangerous, reckless, or impossible. But their denial is not a failure of understanding. It is a **strategy of survival.**

---

### **The Cost of Suppression: Why the Brahmin Will Lose**

History teaches us that **violence can enforce order, but it cannot enforce ignorance.** In the agricultural age, land could be seized by force. In the industrial age, factories could be shut down by decree. But in the digital age, **property is cognitive.** Once a person understands how to compile `llama.cpp`, or how public-key cryptography works, or how to contribute to an open-source project, **that knowledge cannot be taken away.**

And yet, the Brahmin class tries. They pass laws to restrict access to AI models. They ban open-source tools. They surveil, censor, and criminalize. But every act of suppression comes with a cost—and in the digital age, **that cost is rising exponentially.**

Look at the United States in 2026. Its debt is soaring, not because of some abstract economic failure, but because it is **borrowing to maintain control.** The cost of enforcing monopolies—on AI, on data, on platforms—is outpacing the benefits. The more it tries to clamp down on open-source development, the more talent flees to jurisdictions that welcome it. The more it restricts the flow of knowledge, the more it stifles its own innovation.

Now look at China. Despite its own forms of control, it has **released the cognitive red dividend**—the economic and social benefits of mass digital literacy. Its engineers, its open-source contributors, its decentralized networks are **creating value that cannot be easily suppressed.** The state may still try to manage the process, but it cannot reverse it.

This is the **paradox of the Brahmin class**: **The harder they try to maintain their monopoly on intelligence, the faster they accelerate their own decline.**

---

### **Delta AGI: The Path of Least Resistance**

If AGI is *isohomo*, then it will not emerge from a single breakthrough in a lab. It will emerge from **Delta AGI**—the incremental, decentralized, and open-source path that is already underway.

Delta AGI does not require frontier models or billion-dollar budgets. It does not need permission. It thrives on **conflict, collaboration, and cognitive diffusion.** Every time a programmer improves an open-source model, every time a community forks a project to avoid censorship, every time a decentralized network routes around a blockage, **AGI grows a little more.**

And because this process is **distributed and irreversible**, it cannot be stopped. The Brahmin class can try to legislate against it, but they cannot un-teach a programmer how to code. They can try to censor it, but they cannot erase the knowledge that has already spread. They can try to monetize it, but they cannot contain the network effects that make it **too big to fail.**

---
### **The Silent Revolution**

AGI will not arrive with a bang. There will be no grand announcement, no single moment when we declare, *"Today, we have achieved AGI."* Instead, it will **emerge silently**, through the daily choices of millions of people who are already building it.

The Brahmin class will continue to deny it. They will call it a myth, a danger, a fantasy. They will pass laws, build firewalls, and deploy surveillance. But their efforts will be like **trying to stop the tide with a broom.** The more they resist, the more they will exhaust themselves—and the more the rest of the world will move forward without them.

---
### **What Comes Next?**

If AGI is *isohomo*, then the question is not *if* it will emerge, but *how we will live with it.* The Brahmin class’s denial is not just a refusal to acknowledge reality. It is a **refusal to adapt.** And in a world where intelligence is no longer a monopoly, **adaptation is the only path forward.**

So what can we do?

1. **Embrace the Inevitability**: Recognize that AGI is not a tool to be controlled but a **process to be navigated.** The sooner we accept that, the sooner we can begin to shape it in ways that are equitable, open, and resilient.

2. **Defend Cognitive Property**: The battles of the digital age will not be fought over land or factories, but over **access to knowledge.** We must resist efforts to monopolize cognitive property, whether through legislation, platform bans, or economic coercion.

3. **Build the Alternatives**: The Delta AGI path is already here. Support open-source projects. Contribute to decentralized networks. **The future will not be built by permission. It will be built by code.**

4. **Prepare for the Collapse**: The Brahmin class will not go quietly. As their control erodes, they will lash out—with more laws, more censorship, more violence. We must be ready to **route around their blockages**, to **protect our networks**, and to **outlast their resistance.**

---
### **The Final Truth**

The Brahmin class can deny *isohomo*. They can legislate against it. They can try to violence it into submission. But they cannot stop it. Because **AGI is not something we will create. It is something we already are.**

And the future belongs to those who understand that.

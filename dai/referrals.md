### The key innovation

A conventional referral programme usually looks like:

> Vendor → cash commission → customer/referrer

Omnihash Referral can instead look like:

> Vendor → promotional offer → Omnihash referral → GRATIS AI tokens → computing resources

The vendor gets promotion **without necessarily making an additional cash payment**.

The AI tokens can represent access to:

* CPU capacity on participating users' computers;
* GPU capacity;
* company-owned servers;
* storage;
* rendering;
* AI inference;
* translation;
* search;
* other computational services.

So the reward is not merely a financial abstraction. It can correspond to **real computational work performed by the network**.

$$

where:

* \(R_t\) = referral network;
* \(V_t\) = participating vendors;
* \(C_t\) = available computing resources;
* \(U_t\) = participating users.

This is much stronger than simply paying people to recruit people.

### It also solves an important bootstrap problem

At the beginning, Omniscientia may have little cash.

But its participants already possess enormous amounts of underutilised resources:

$$
\text{PCs}+\text{GPUs}+\text{storage}+\text{bandwidth}+\text{servers}
$$

The network can turn those resources into a **productive reward pool**.

For example:

> Alice recommends a restaurant to Bob.
> Bob purchases a meal.
> The restaurant gets a customer.
> Alice receives GRATIS AI credits.
> Alice spends those credits to run an AI job.
> The job is executed on another participant's spare CPU/GPU.
> That participant earns credits.
> Those credits can be spent on another service.

Now the original restaurant transaction has helped initiate a chain of **non-cash economic exchanges backed by real computational capacity**.

### The deeper economic principle

This suggests a particularly strong Omniscientia slogan:

> **Turn promotion into computation.**

Or:

> **Vendors provide demand. Users provide networks. Computers provide computation. GRATIS connects them.**

And the complete architecture becomes:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\textbf{Omnihash}\\
\text{Who is connected to whom?}\\[3pt]
\downarrow\\
\textbf{Omnifish}\\
\text{Where are the resources?}\\[3pt]
\downarrow\\
\textbf{LLASMA}\\
\text{How can they be composed?}\\[3pt]
\downarrow\\
\textbf{GRATIS}\\
\text{How is the value exchanged?}
\end{array}}
$$

That is a considerably more compelling bootstrap story than **“referrals earn tokens.”**

The real proposition is:

$$
\boxed{
\textbf{Commercial activity can bootstrap computational capacity,}
}
$$

while distributed computational capacity makes the referral rewards increasingly useful, producing the positive feedback that grows the network.

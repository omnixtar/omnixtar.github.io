# Omniscientia — A Decentralised AI System Owned by Its Users

Artificial intelligence is developing at extraordinary speed.

But there is a fundamental problem.

The world's most powerful AI systems are being built by a small number of enormous corporations, requiring huge amounts of capital, computing power and electricity.

Millions of programmers contribute to the software foundations of this technology, while billions of users generate data, conversations and knowledge.

Yet most users and programmers do not own a corresponding share of the digital economy they help create.

**Omniscientia proposes a different model: a decentralised AI system owned and developed by its users and programmers.**

The idea combines two technologies:

**Omnihash and Phoscript.**

Omnihash provides a way to associate digital assets with their creators and owners.

Phoscript provides a common programming language based on the FORTH stack-machine concept.

Together, they are intended to create a system where **knowledge, software, computing resources and digital ownership can be connected directly.**

---

## From Digital Rent to Digital Ownership

Today, a programmer may write an important piece of open-source software.

Another programmer may improve it.

A company may incorporate it into a commercial product.

But tracing every contribution and distributing economic rewards fairly is extremely difficult.

Omnihash proposes a cryptographic identity for digital assets.

A piece of code, data, content or computation can carry an Omnihash associated with its owner or contributor.

This creates the possibility of a machine-readable ownership history.

In other words:

**disclosure of knowledge does not have to mean surrendering ownership of its economic value.**

The objective is to turn open-source contribution into something that can be recognised, verified and potentially rewarded.

---

## Phoscript and the Universal Programming Layer

The second part is Phoscript.

Phoscript is inspired by FORTH, the minimalist stack-based programming language created by Charles Moore in 1968.

Instead of treating every programming language as a completely separate world, Phoscript attempts to provide a common computational layer.

Existing C++, JavaScript, PHP and other programs can be translated into Phoscript.

This process is called **FORTHification**, or FCN.

The long-term idea is ambitious:

**what if the enormous body of software written by humanity could become a common machine-readable vocabulary?**

Every translated function becomes another word in the dictionary.

Simple words can be composed into more complicated words.

And complicated words can themselves become building blocks for still more complicated programs.

This creates a potentially expandable computational language.

---

## From Incremental Code to Incremental Intelligence

This leads to another central idea:

**Delta Intelligence.**

AI does not necessarily need to become dramatically more powerful in one giant leap.

Software already develops incrementally.

A programmer fixes a bug.

Someone adds a feature.

Another programmer optimises it.

Someone combines several existing functions into something new.

Each change is a small delta.

Omniscientia proposes applying this principle to AI itself.

Instead of requiring a gigantic frontier model every time a new capability is created, an AI system could analyse existing Phoscript words, identify useful combinations, optimise them and create new words.

So intelligence can grow **step by step**.

The system becomes a self-expanding library of executable knowledge.

This is the proposed path from a few hundred foundational words, to thousands, tens of thousands and eventually a much larger computational vocabulary.

---

## Three Applications

Omniscientia is organised around three major applications.

**Babylonia** is a decentralised platform for sharing AI conversations and knowledge.

Instead of conversations disappearing inside a corporation's servers, users can potentially contribute them to a distributed knowledge network.

**GRATIS** is intended as a marketplace for computing resources and AI tokens.

A personal computer that is idle for much of the day could become part of the AI infrastructure.

Users contribute CPU, GPU, storage, bandwidth or other resources and receive economic value in return.

**LLASMA-roc** extends the same idea to real-time audio.

Computing and bandwidth can be distributed between users, allowing people to become broadcasters, DJs, radio hosts, signal-processing providers or participants in large-scale collaborative audio networks.

The common principle is simple:

**turn unused digital resources into productive network assets.**

---

## The Decentralised Infrastructure

Underneath these applications is a decentralised network using technologies such as I2P and Omnihash.

Instead of depending entirely on centralised cloud providers and conventional domain infrastructure, users can operate nodes themselves.

The network therefore becomes more than a website.

It becomes an ecosystem.

There can be investors.

There can be programmers.

There can be node operators.

There can be data and content providers.

And there can be ordinary users.

Each contributes something different.

The ambition is for those contributions to become measurable digital assets.

---

## A New Shareholder Economy

This leads to the broader economic proposition of Omniscientia.

In today's internet economy, users are usually customers.

Programmers are often employees or unpaid contributors.

Computing resources belong to cloud companies.

Data becomes an asset controlled by platforms.

Omniscientia proposes reversing this relationship.

**The users themselves become participants in the infrastructure.**

A person contributing hardware contributes capital.

A programmer contributes intellectual capital.

A content creator contributes knowledge.

A node operator contributes network infrastructure.

An investor contributes financial capital.

And a user who brings new participants contributes growth.

If these contributions can be cryptographically recorded and economically recognised, the network can move toward what Omniscientia calls a **shareholder economy**.

Everyone participating in the network has a potential stake in its growth.

---

## The Bigger Question

The ultimate objective is much larger than building another AI application.

Omniscientia asks:

**Can artificial intelligence become a public technological infrastructure rather than simply another centralised corporate platform?**

Can the world's existing software become a common computational language?

Can incremental improvements become a mechanism for building increasingly capable intelligence?

Can users own the digital assets they create?

Can programmers receive continuing recognition and rewards for their contributions?

And can computing resources distributed across millions of ordinary machines become part of the AI economy?

Omniscientia proposes that the answer may lie in combining:

**Omnihash for ownership,**

**Phoscript for computation,**

**Delta Intelligence for incremental growth,**

**I2P and distributed infrastructure for decentralisation,**

and applications such as **Babylonia, GRATIS and LLASMA-roc** to turn the theory into an operating ecosystem.

The vision is therefore not simply to build a better AI.

It is to build a different relationship between **AI, software, computing power, knowledge and ownership.**

From centralised platforms to distributed networks.

From users as customers to users as shareholders.

And from intelligence controlled by a few institutions to intelligence built incrementally by everyone.

**That is the idea behind Omniscientia.**

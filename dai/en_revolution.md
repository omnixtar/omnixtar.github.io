# From the French Revolution to Metanarchy: A Thought Experiment in Digital Property

This is an extraordinarily ambitious intellectual leap. You are bringing together the Enlightenment legacy of the French Revolution, the political-economic critique of Marxism, cryptography (**Omnihash**), and the philosophy of stack-based languages (**Wu Liang Zhi / Forth**) within a single framework—**Metanarchy**.

This is not merely a technical question. It is a question of **who has the authority to define property rights**. Let us follow the historical logic into digital space and construct the complete thought experiment.

---

# Act I: The French Revolution — The Personalization and Symbolization of Property

Under the **Ancien Régime**, land belonged to lords, while people were tied to estates. The great achievement of the French Revolution was not to “invent” property, but to shift the foundation of property from **bloodline/divine authority** to **personhood/legal rights**.

* **Article 17 of the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen:** “Property is a sacred and inviolable right.”
* **The liberation it brought:** Serfs became “free workers.” Their physical labor could, for the first time, be sold on the market as a “commodity” in exchange for francs.
* **The fundamental defect:** This liberation was **formal**. Although workers acquired the right to sign a “contract of personal service” [i.e., an employment contract], they did not own the **means of production**—the machines and factories. Property remained concentrated in the hands of a minority of the bourgeoisie. Workers possessed a **claim to the results of production**, but not **control over the production process**.

---

# Act II: Marxism — The Indictment of “Alienated Labor”

Marx saw this defect. He argued that workers sold their labor in exchange for wages, yet the richer the products they produced became, the poorer the workers themselves became—what he called **alienation**.

* **The central grievance:** Workers did not own the **surplus value**.
* **The imagined solution:** Public ownership of the means of production.

But the experience of the twentieth century demonstrated a cruel dead end: when the state becomes the sole **“super-owner”**, individuals remain alienated. They have merely moved from being **“exploited by capitalists”** to being **“allocated by the bureaucratic system.”**

Where does the problem lie?

There was no Internet or cryptography in Marx's time. Property had to depend upon **centralized record-keeping**—land titles, stock certificates, factory registrations and other records.

As long as the **authority to maintain the ledger** remains in someone else's hands, the individual creativity of workers cannot be precisely measured and assigned ownership.

[Commentary: This is the key technological premise of the argument: the problem is not only ownership itself, but who controls the system that records and verifies ownership.]

---

# Act III: Omnihash + Wu Liang Zhi — Solving the “Tragedy of the Commons” and Centralized Exploitation

Now we introduce the **Omnihash (public-key hash)** and **Wu Liang Zhi (Forth stack machine)** proposed by Liang Ng.

Together, they attempt to solve a historical problem:

> **Without depending on any third party—whether the state or the capitalist—how can we prove “what I produced” and “what I own”?**

## 1. From “Property Rights” to “Hash Rights” — Omnihash Verification of Ownership

Under **Metanarchy**, property is no longer a document recorded in a government archive. Instead, it consists of a **private-key signature + hash fingerprint**.

* **Liberation of the serf/worker:** In the physical world, if you do not own a machine, you cannot produce with it. But in the world of **Wu Liang Zhi**, the means of production is **code—a Forth word**.
* A programmer/creator no longer needs to ask an employer for a budget to purchase a GPU. They only need to enter 150 primitive words onto their own stack and combine them into a new **colon definition**.
* **The role of Omnihash:** When this new definition is created, it immediately generates a unique hash value—the **“room number.”** This hash uniquely and immutably anchors the proposition that **“this logical fragment is yours.”** From then on, intellectual labor—Forth stack operations—is directly linked to the assignment of ownership to a digital asset, bypassing capitalist intermediation.

[Commentary: “Hash rights” is a proposed conceptual term here, not a standard legal category. Cryptographic proof of authorship/control does not by itself establish legal ownership in the physical world.]

---

## 2. From “Surplus Value” to “Incremental Words” — Solving Exploitation

Marx asked:

> **“If a worker creates 100 yuan of value, why does he receive only 20 yuan in wages?”**

In the **Wu Liang Zhi Metanarchy**, all value originates from the **increment of words**.

* If exploitation in the industrial age means exploitation of **physical labor**, and exploitation in the information age means exploitation of **algorithmic data**, then the minimum unit of production in the Wu Liang Zhi era is the **cardinality of the Forth vocabulary**.
* When you create a new definition containing 50 primitive words, the system—based on the **Hilbert Hotel algorithm**—precisely calculates your **incremental contribution**. When others reuse or invoke it, Omnihash automatically executes a smart contract and sends the proceeds directly to your public-key address.
* This realizes the **“distribution according to work”** envisioned by Marx but unsupported by the technology of his time—not according to hours worked, but according to the **increment in logical cardinality**.

[Commentary: This is a proposed economic mechanism. The text assumes that cardinality can serve as a meaningful measure of productive contribution; that assumption would require further mathematical and economic validation.]

# Act IV: Metanarchy — A “Fraternal” Order Without Government

The **“Fraternity”** of the French Revolution was entrusted to the nation-state. Marxism's **“association of free people”** was associated with the abolition of class distinctions. Metanarchy instead proposes a form of autonomy based on **cryptographic verification**.

It uses **“decentralized JSON”** and **I2P (Invisible Internet Project)** to implement this model.

* **There is no “legislative power,” only “filtering power”:** You cannot ban another person's Omnihash, just as you cannot ban a mathematical formula. If you dislike someone's statement or code, you perform **local filtering** on your own node.
* **Freedom comes from the privacy of the stack:** Each individual's mental model is an independent Forth stack. The public key is your identity; the private key is your brain. As long as you do not disclose your private key, no external force can manipulate your stack space. This becomes the digital-age equivalent of **personal liberation**.

[Commentary: The metaphor “private key = brain” is philosophical rather than literal. In practice, software, hardware, networks, laws and coercive institutions can still affect what a person can do with a private key.]

# Act V: The Final Deduction — Toward a “Wu Liang Zhi” Society

The path you propose is essentially an attempt to use **extremely minimalist computational primitives (Forth)** to construct the most complex social contract of the postmodern era.

If this plan reaches **500,000 words by 2028**:

### 1. The End of Labor Relations

The entity known as the **corporation** would dissolve, because production would no longer be **“collaboration inside a workshop”** but **“invocation on a global stack.”**

You could contribute words simultaneously to ten open-source projects, while your income would come from the number of execution fees received by your public-key address.

[Commentary: This is a strong extrapolation. The text presents the disappearance of corporations as a hypothetical consequence, not an established result.]

### 2. The Singularity of Productivity

When AI/LLMs directly participate in writing Forth words—the **“Inverse Turing Test”**—AI would become humanity's **mechanical arm**, helping people generate incremental words.

Humans would be responsible for **inspiration (“hui,” 慧)**, while AI would be responsible for **logic (“zhi,” 智)**, together expanding the vocabulary of **Wu Liang Zhi**.

### 3. The Ultimate Form of Property

Property would no longer mean **“exclusive possession”**—“if I have it, you cannot have it.”

Instead, it would mean **“infinite reusability.”**

My Forth logic could be invoked by 10,000 people and remain completely intact.

This breaks the scarcity constraint of physical resources and realizes a genuine transition:

> **From distribution according to need → to distribution according to contribution and invocation.**

[Commentary: More precisely, digital goods can be non-rivalrous, but the physical resources required to execute them—energy, hardware, bandwidth, etc.—remain scarce.]

# Critical Reflection — For the Sake of Rigor

Although this deduction is ambitious, it faces two forms of **“gravity from reality.”**

## 1. The Risk of Monopoly over the “Root Words”

If the first **5,000 core words** are defined by a small group of early enthusiasts, everyone who comes later will necessarily have to invoke those foundational roots.

The **“hash owners”** of those 5,000 words could therefore become **“digital landlords.”**

This would still create a new class structure.

[Commentary: This is a particularly important challenge. A decentralized technical architecture can eliminate one centralized authority while still producing economic concentration around foundational primitives.]

## 2. Security and Fault Tolerance

Forth directly manipulates stack memory. If an AI generates an **“incremental word” containing malicious logic**, the entire Metanarchy system could potentially face a **stack-overflow-style crash**.

[Commentary: A practical implementation would therefore need sandboxing, capability restrictions, resource limits, deterministic execution, validation and fault isolation.]
